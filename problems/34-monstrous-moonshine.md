# 34 · Monstrous moonshine

**Tracks:** Algebra/Number Theory, Computation/Applied Math · **Area:** Modular forms, theoretical physics

## Description

Monstrous moonshine is an incredible connection between the Monster group, the largest sporadic simple group, and modular functions, through the lens of string theory. The goal is to find a new proof.

## Desired outcome

A novel proof of a central component, such as a denominator identity, a replication relation, or a key step toward the genus-zero property.

## References

- **J. H. Conway and S. P. Norton (1979).** *Monstrous Moonshine*. Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society, 11(3), 308–339. Original formulation of the Monstrous Moonshine conjectures, connecting the Monster sporadic simple group to modular functions of genus zero. <https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/11.3.308>
- **I. Frenkel, J. Lepowsky, and A. Meurman (1988).** *Vertex Operator Algebras and the Monster*. Pure and Applied Mathematics, Vol. 134, Academic Press. Construction of the Moonshine module, an infinite-dimensional graded representation of the Monster group. <https://shop.elsevier.com/books/vertex-operator-algebras-and-the-monster/frenkel/978-0-12-267065-7>
- **R. E. Borcherds (1992).** *Monstrous Moonshine and Monstrous Lie Superalgebras*. Inventiones Mathematicae, 109, 405–444. Original proof using generalized Kac–Moody algebras, denominator identities, and the no-ghost theorem from string theory. Particularly relevant are Sections 6–9 and Theorem 1.1. <https://math.berkeley.edu/~reb/papers/monster/monster.pdf>
