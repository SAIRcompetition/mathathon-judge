- **Area:** Group Theory, Combinatorial Group Theory, Geometric Group Theory

### Description

Prove that there exists an infinite group G generated by two elements and satisfying the identity x^n = 1 for some positive integer n. Determine as many values of n as possible for which such a group exists. In particular, investigate the free Burnside groups B(2,n), their infinitude, and the dependence of the answer on the exponent n.

### Desired outcome

A rigorous proof of the existence of an infinite two-generated group of finite exponent, together with an explicit description of the construction. Participants should identify as many admissible exponents as possible, explain which cases are known, and, if feasible, improve existing bounds or develop alternative proofs.

### Notes

- Adian (2015) outlines a modification of the Novikov–Adian theory giving infinite $B(m,n)$ for odd $n\ge101$; the paper presents this as an outline, with a full proof to appear separately. Atkarskaya–Rips–Tent describe 665 as the previous best odd bound and lower it to 557; they do not cite the 2015 outline.

### The result

The free Burnside group is
$$B(m,n)=F_m/\langle\!\langle w^n : w\in F_m\rangle\!\rangle .$$
Every $m$-generated group of exponent dividing $n$ is a quotient of it, so $B(2,n)$ is infinite exactly when some infinite 2-generated group of exponent dividing $n$ exists. $B(m,n)$ is finite for $n=2,3,4,6$ (Burnside, Sanov, M. Hall). For $m\ge2$ it is infinite for odd $n\ge4381$ (Novikov–Adian 1968), odd $n\ge665$ (Adian 1979), $n>2^{48}$ (Ivanov 1994), all $n\ge8000$ (with Lysenok 1996), and odd $n\ge557$ (Atkarskaya–Rips–Tent, preprint). Whether $B(2,5)$, $B(2,7)$ or $B(2,8)$ is infinite is open.

### How the known proof works

Structure of the Atkarskaya–Rips–Tent proof (Sections 1–2):
- Define a rank on words: $w$ has rank at least $k+1$ if it cyclically contains $v^\tau$ with $v$ of rank at least $k$, for a fixed nesting constant $\tau$. Put $N_k=\langle\!\langle w^n:\mathrm{rk}(w)\le k\rangle\!\rangle$, an ascending chain with union $\langle\!\langle w^n\rangle\!\rangle$.
- By induction on $k$, define a canonical form $\mathrm{can}_k(w)$ for each coset of $N_k$, using "certification sequences" that choose sides of relators; the induction carries 13 hypotheses.
- Canonical forms stabilize in $k$, giving a section $B(m,n)\to F_m$.
- Every cube-free word is canonical, and there are infinitely many cube-free words on two letters. The method needs $n>36\cdot15+16=556$.
- For comparison, the Novikov–Adian proof uses an induction with 178 assumptions; Ol'shanskii's van Kampen diagram proof is shorter but needs $n>10^{10}$.

### Background

- Golod and Shafarevich (1964) gave infinite finitely generated torsion groups of unbounded exponent.
- The restricted Burnside problem (finitely many finite $m$-generated groups of exponent $n$) was solved positively by Zelmanov (1989).
- Other geometric approaches for large odd exponents are due to Delzant–Gromov and Coulon. Infinite free Burnside groups are not finitely presented; the Atkarskaya–Rips–Tent proof also gives this.

### Where to start

- [The Burnside problem for odd exponents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15997), A. Atkarskaya, E. Rips, K. Tent, 2023: a comparatively short proof for odd $n\ge557$.

Prerequisites: free groups and presentations, combinatorics on words, small cancellation theory and van Kampen diagrams.
