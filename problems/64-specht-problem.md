# 64 · The Specht problem

- **Status:** Part (a) was proved by Belov (1999–2000), with related examples by Grishin and Shchigolev; part (b) was proved by Kemer (1990, English translation 1991).
- **Task:** Infinitely based identities in characteristic $p$; finite bases for boundedly many variables.
- **Tracks:** Algebra/Number Theory · **Area:** Associative Algebra, Polynomial Identity Theory (PI-Algebras), Universal Algebra
- **Key reference:** [Counterexamples to the Specht problem](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/sm460), A. Ya. Belov, Sb. Math. 191 (2000), 329–340.
- **Lean:** Official statement.

## Description

A polynomial identity of an associative algebra is a noncommutative polynomial that vanishes under every substitution of algebra elements.

(a) Construct an infinite system of polynomial identities that is not finitely based, working over a field of positive characteristic.

(b) Prove that, over an infinite field, if the total number of distinct variables occurring in a system of polynomial identities is bounded, then the entire system follows from a finite subsystem.

Investigate the connection with Specht's problem and T-ideals.

## Desired outcome

An explicit example of an infinitely based system of polynomial identities in an associative algebra, with a rigorous proof that no finite subsystem generates all the identities. Participants should also prove the finite basis property for systems involving a bounded number of variables under appropriate assumptions on the base field. Alternative constructions and extensions to different characteristics are welcome.

## The result

Specht (1950) asked whether every system of polynomial identities of associative algebras in characteristic 0 follows from a finite subsystem, i.e. whether every T-ideal is finitely based. Kemer (1987) proved this in characteristic 0. (a) Belov (2000) constructed infinitely based varieties over infinite fields of every positive characteristic. For $p>2$ and $q=p^s$, $s>1$, the identities
$$R_n=[[u,t],t]\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_i^{p-1}y_i^{p-1}[x_i,y_i]\,\big([t,[t,v]][[u,t],t]\big)^{q-1}[t,[t,v]]=0,\qquad n\ge1,$$
are not finitely based (form quoted in Drensky's survey, Theorem 9.1; a similar family exists for $p=2$). (b) Kemer (Izv. 1990; English 1991): over an infinite field every finitely generated PI-algebra has the same identities as some finite-dimensional algebra; consequently a free associative algebra of finite rank satisfies the ascending chain condition on T-ideals.

## How the known proof works

- (a) Belov's starting point (Section 1) is Grishin's and Shchigolev's infinitely based T-spaces in positive characteristic, for example Shchigolev's system $Q(x_1,y_1)\cdots Q(x_n,y_n)$ with $Q(x,y)=x^{p-1}y^{p-1}[x,y]$; the aim is to transfer these to T-ideals.
- (a) Section 2 works with an auxiliary algebra generated by $x_i,y_i$ in the variety defined by $[[x,y],z]=0$, in which words with $p+1$ occurrences of one generator vanish (Grishin's algebra when $p=2$).
- (b) In characteristic $p$ Kemer replaces multilinearization by quasi-linearization: partial linearizations, with degrees that are powers of $p$ (Belov-Kanel–Rowen–Vishne, §1.4).
- (b) The main step is representability: a finitely generated PI-algebra over an infinite field has the same identities as a finite-dimensional algebra. The proof uses Kemer polynomials and Kemer's lemmas on the semisimple part and the nilpotency index of the radical.
- (b) Representability gives the ascending chain condition on T-ideals of a free algebra of finite rank, which is statement (b).

## Background

- Kemer's characteristic-0 proof passes from superalgebras to all algebras through the Grassmann envelope and representations of symmetric groups; that last step fails in characteristic $p$.
- All known counterexamples in characteristic $p$ are infinitely generated, consistent with (b). Belov extended (b) to finitely generated PI-algebras over any commutative Noetherian ring, including finite fields; Belov-Kanel, Rowen and Vishne wrote out the proof in full.
- Other examples: Grishin (characteristic 2, nil of index 32), Shchigolev (monomial identities), Gupta–Krasilnikov (characteristic 2, perhaps the simplest). Sandu (2005) built infinite independent systems of identities.
