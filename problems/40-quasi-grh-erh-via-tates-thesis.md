## Notes

- Hecke extensions already exist in claimed form: the 7/8 theorem covers Hecke $L$-functions over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$; OpenAI's Artin primitive-root paper (October 2026, Theorem 1.2) gives $\operatorname{Re}s>1-10^{-6}$ for finite-order Hecke $L$-functions over cyclotomic fields containing $\mu_{12}$; Cureton and Thorner (Hexagon preprint, October 2026) claim $\zeta_K(s)\neq0$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>31/32$ for every number field $K$, and the same for finite-order Hecke $L$-functions over $K$. None is refereed.
- The description's extension "by AxiomMath" to Dedekind zeta functions is the Cureton–Thorner report, whose proofs were generated by AxiomMath's AxiomProver.

## Description

A surprising result in OpenAI's recent cache of theorems is that of quasi-GRH, though in the style of much of AI math, the resulting proof was very formula-heavy. Much of the proof is implicitly operating in the realm of Tate's Thesis, and subsequent extensions to Dedekind Zeta Functions by AxiomMath further solidify these connections. This project would seek to give a refined exposition of OpenAI's proof explicitly in this setting, trying to replace as many technical steps with more conceptually motivated/natural adelic arguments.

## Desired outcome

A shortened exposition of a proof of quasi-GRH, with extensions to Hecke L-functions or identified obstructions to these extensions. Explicit separation of purely analytic inputs and adelic machinery, trying to minimize these analytic inputs.

## The result

OpenAI's Theorem 1.1: every finite-order Hecke $L$-function over $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ and every Dirichlet $L$-function has no zero in $\operatorname{Re}s>7/8$ (the pole at $s=1$ allowed). In particular
$$\zeta(s)\neq0\qquad(\operatorname{Re}s>7/8),$$
the quasi-Riemann hypothesis. Corollary 1.2 bounds the least quadratic non-residue by $C(\log p)^A$. A separate 49-page OpenAI paper (5 October 2026, written with human assistance) proves the same for $\operatorname{Re}s>11/12$ by a shorter route.

## How the known proof works

- Continuation criterion (Prop. 2.1): for each Hecke character $\eta$, a character sum is bounded directly and compared with a Mellin integral of $H_\eta(s)/L^S_F(s,\eta)$; power savings in both continue $1/L_F$ past the rightmost zeros.
- The sum averages Fourier coefficients of Kubota–Patterson cubic theta series against sextic residue characters; it is computed twice, by a cubic reflection (Prop. 5.1) and by Poisson summation, whose row $u=1$ contains $1/L_F(s,\eta)$.
- Part I (11/12): quadratic and planar additive large sieves bound the reflected side; a zero detector with the sextic large sieve counts rows with zeros.
- Part II (7/8): prime compensation, unequal scales and two new moment estimates proved by induction (Lemmas 17.1, 18.1); this takes most of the 199 pages.
- Transfer: $\chi\circ N_{F/\mathbb Q}$ is a Hecke character whose $L$-function is $L(s,\chi)L(s,\chi\chi_{-3})$ up to finitely many Euler factors.

## Background

- Classical zero-free regions shrink toward $\operatorname{Re}s=1$ as height or conductor grow.
- The adelic version is in OpenAI's primitive-root paper (§§3–8). Cureton–Thorner (§2.3) list six ingredients of that argument: Tate's thesis supplies the Fourier analysis behind the Poisson evaluation and the functional equations, and Kazhdan–Patterson's cubic exceptional representation the theta function.
