# Mathathon Round 1: Judge Rules

In Round 1 you score the presentations of the teams assigned to you, together with their Q&A. Separately, a panel of seven AI models (the LLM panel) reviews each team's written report. Its scores are advisory. Scores are used only to build a shortlist, and the judges decide the winners.

You take three steps on the SAIR competition platform: registration (§1), problem selection (§2), and scoring (§3). The LLM judge is described in §4. How to score, with the guidelines and the rubric, is in the [Judge Guidelines](judge-guidelines.md). The participants' rules are in [Participant Rules](participant-rules.md). All times are Pacific Time.

## 1. Registration

- **When.** Before Nov 13.
- **Invitation only.** The organizers appoint the judges. Open the invitation email or the competition page, then sign up or log in with your SAIR ID.
- **Judge agreement.** Accept it on the platform. It includes the conflict-of-interest rules (§2) and the independence rule (§3.1). The review entry then appears on the website.

## 2. Problem selection

- **When.** Complete the initial survey by Nov 11. Submit your updated problem preference through SAIR's platform by Nov 13.
- **Initial Survey.** From the list of proposed problems, select any that you would be interested in judging. You are not expected to be an expert in a subject area, only to have some familiarity with and interest in the topic. This survey allows us to choose a final list of problems for the competition. Please complete the survey by midnight Nov 11.
- **Preference Updates.** Your responses to the initial survey will be added to the final problem list on SAIR's platform. You are free to update this selection until midnight Nov 13.
- **Balancing.** The organizers start from your choices and then balance the assignments so that every presentation has enough judges. You may be assigned a problem you did not choose.
- **Your presentations.** The organizers assign presentations after teams' problems are locked, at the problem selection deadline [TBD]. Assignments are final before the first session. Each presentation has 2 to 5 judges, including at least one from the team's field when possible, and you have at most one presentation per session. Each assigned presentation shows the team's problem and slides. You may access teams' report and supplementary information after judging their presentation.
- **Conflicts of interest.** Tell the organizers about any relationship with a team that could affect impartiality.
  - **Recuse** from a team if you have a close personal or professional relationship with one of its members, such as family, an advisor or advisee, the same research group, or a close collaborator. Also recuse if you helped the team substantively during the event.
  - If you are assigned a team you have a conflict with, tell the organizers before the presentation.

## 3. Scoring

- **When.** Scoring opens at 12:00 PM on Nov 15, when you get an email, and closes at the end of the last session (3:30 PM).
- **What you score.** Only the presentations assigned to you: the presentation and its Q&A. After the presentation scores are submitted you may read teams' full reports.

### 3.1 At each presentation

- **Independence.** Do not look at any LLM output until you have submitted all your scores. You do not receive LLM-suggested questions before presentations.
- **Shared problems.** You will be told which teams worked on the same problem.
- **Questions.** Suggested questions are in the [Judge Guidelines](judge-guidelines.md#4-questions-to-ask).

### 3.2 The scoring form

Fill in one form for each presentation, on the platform:

- Correctness, Clarity, Motivation, and Interest: a whole number from 1 to 5 each, following the [Judge Guidelines](judge-guidelines.md), with at least one sentence of justification;
- shortlist nomination: yes or no, with a one-sentence reason if yes;
- feedback for the team;
- whether the project should continue in Round 2: yes, maybe, or no;
- any conflict of interest.

- **When to submit.** Right after the presentation, and no later than the end of the last session.
- **Drafts and edits.** You can save drafts, and you can edit your submitted scores until scoring closes. After that, a score can be changed only if the organizers reopen it.
- **Privacy.** Your scores, justifications, and feedback are sent to the team privately. They are never published.

## 4. LLM judge

- **The panel.** The LLM judge is a panel of seven AI models: Claude Opus 5.5, GPT 6.1 sol, Gemini 4, DeepSeek 4.1 Flash, Kimi K3.1, Muse Spark (open), and Mistral Large 4.
- **What it reads.** Each model reads the problem statement and its references, the team's report without its title block, and the results of the automated Lean check if the team submitted a Lean project. The models do not see the slides, the supplementary materials, or the presentation.
- **What it scores.** Correctness, Clarity, Motivation, and Interest, with the same guidelines and rubric as you ([Judge Guidelines](judge-guidelines.md)), plus Attribution and Novelty.
- **Advisory only.** LLM scores do not replace yours, and the judges decide the winners. An issue raised by a model counts against a team only after a human has checked it.
- **Independence.** You see no LLM output until you have submitted all your scores (§3.1).
