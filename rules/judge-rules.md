# Mathathon Round 1: Judge Rules

In Round 1 you score the presentations of the teams assigned to you, together with their Q&A. Separately, a panel of seven AI models (the LLM panel) reviews each team's written report. Its scores are advisory. The judges decide the winners.

You take three steps on the SAIR competition platform: registration (§1), problem preferences (§2), and scoring (§3). The LLM panel is described in §4. How to score, with the guidelines and the rubric, is in the [Judge Guidelines](judge-guidelines.md). The participants' rules are in [Participant Rules](participant-rules.md). All times are Pacific Time.

## 1. Registration

- **When.** Before Nov 13.
- **Invitation only.** The organizers appoint the judges. Open the invitation email or the competition page, then sign up or log in with your SAIR ID.
- **Judge agreement.** Accept it on the platform. It includes the conflict-of-interest rules (§2) and the independence rule (§3.1). The review entry then appears on the website.

## 2. Problem preferences

- **When.** Complete the initial survey by midnight Nov 11. Then update your preferences on the SAIR platform by midnight Nov 13.
- **Initial survey.** From the list of candidate problems, select any that you would be interested in judging. You are not expected to be an expert in the area, only to have some familiarity with and interest in the topic. The organizers use the survey to choose the final problem list.
- **Rating on the platform.** Your survey answers are carried over to the final problem list on the platform. Until midnight Nov 13 you can update them: rate each problem you are able to judge from 1 (willing) to 5 (most preferred), and leave problems you cannot judge at 0. You can also describe, in the optional text box, the kinds of problems you would most like to judge.
- **Balancing.** The organizers start from your ratings and then balance the assignments so that every presentation has enough judges. You may be assigned a problem you did not rate.
- **Presentations you judge.** The organizers plan presentations after teams' problems are locked, on Nov 14 at 11:59 PM, and give you your assignments at the judges' briefing. They appear on the platform after the submission deadline, Nov 15 at 12:00 PM. Assignments are final before the first session. Each presentation has 2 to 5 judges. When possible, at least one of them is an expert in the problem's field. You have at most one presentation per session. Each assigned presentation shows the team's problem and submission. Before you score a presentation, look only at its slides; you may read the report and supplementary materials afterwards.
- **Conflicts of interest.** Tell the organizers about any relationship with a team that could affect impartiality.
  - **Recuse** from a team if you have a close personal or professional relationship with one of its members, such as family, an advisor or advisee, the same research group, or a close collaborator. Also recuse if you helped the team substantively during the event.
  - If you are assigned a team you have a conflict with, tell the organizers before the presentation.

## 3. Scoring

- **When.** Scoring opens when your presentations appear on the platform, after the submission deadline, and closes at the end of the last session, at 3:30 PM.
- **What you score.** Only the presentations assigned to you: the presentation and its Q&A. After you have scored a presentation, you may read the team's full report. The organizers may also give you access to other teams' submissions.

### 3.1 At each presentation

- **Independence.** Do not look at any LLM output until you have submitted all your scores. You do not receive LLM-suggested questions before presentations.
- **Shared problems.** You will be told which teams worked on the same problem.
- **Questions.** Suggested questions are in the [Judge Guidelines](judge-guidelines.md#4-questions-to-ask).

### 3.2 The scoring form

Fill in one form for each presentation, on the platform:

- Correctness, Clarity, Motivation, and Interest: a whole number from 1 to 5 each, following the [Judge Guidelines](judge-guidelines.md), with a comment on each;
- an overall comment.

- **When to submit.** After each presentation, before scoring closes.
- **Drafts and edits.** You can save drafts, and you can edit your submitted scores until scoring closes.
- **Privacy.** Your scores and comments are seen only by the organizers. They are never published.

## 4. LLM panel

- **The panel.** The LLM panel consists of seven AI models: Claude Opus 5.5, GPT 6.1 sol, Gemini 4, DeepSeek 4.1 Flash, Kimi K3.1, Muse Spark (open), and Mistral Large 4.
- **What it reads.** Each model reads the problem statement and its references, the team's report without its title block, any supplementary materials, and the results of the automated Lean check if the team submitted a Lean project. The models do not see the slides or the presentation.
- **What it scores.** Correctness, Clarity, Motivation, and Interest, with the same guidelines and rubric as you ([Judge Guidelines](judge-guidelines.md)), plus Attribution and Novelty.
- **Advisory only.** LLM scores do not replace yours, and the judges decide the winners. An issue raised by a model counts against a team only after a human has checked it.
- **Independence.** You see no LLM output until you have submitted all your scores (§3.1).
