## Title/name of problem

The Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis

## Short description of problem (100 words or less)

The Riemann hypothesis conjectures that all nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function have real part 1/2. A quasi-Riemann hypothesis is a weaker version of this conjecture, a zero-free half-plane of the form Re(s)>sigma, for some sigma < 1. Last month, OpenAI published two papers establishing quasi-Riemann, one asserting a zero-free region Re(s)>11/12 and the other a zero-free region Re(s)>7/8. Much of the proof is implicitly operating in the realm of Tate's Thesis, and subsequent extensions to Dedekind Zeta Functions by AxiomMath further solidify these connections.

## Desired outcome

Mathematicians have been expending great effort to understand the October 5 Quasi-RH paper, which establishes a zero-free half-plane Re(s)>11/12. This paper was written with human assistance and is more digestible than the much longer September 30 paper, which obtains the stronger zero-free half-plane Re(s)>7/8. I hope that participants can simplify and provide better exposition of this nearly 200-page proof, showcasing its key techniques and limitations.

Another goal is a shortened exposition of a proof of quasi-GRH, with extensions to Hecke L-functions or identified obstructions to these extensions. Explicit separation of purely analytic inputs and adelic machinery, trying to minimize these analytic inputs.
