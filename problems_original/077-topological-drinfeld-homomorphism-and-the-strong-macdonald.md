# Problem 77

## Problem ID

77

## Title/name of problem

Topological Drinfeld homomorphism and the strong Macdonald conjecture

## Short description of problem (100 words or less)

For a simply connected space X and a complex reductive algebraic group G, the Drinfeld homomorphism maps a free graded-commutative algebra on selected Hodge components of circle-equivariant free-loop homology of X to G-invariant representation homology of X. Both sides have topological interpretations. Berest, Ramadoss, and Yeung prove this map is an isomorphism for specified spaces using Fishel, Grojnowski, and Teleman’s cohomology results for truncated current Lie algebras and a related Lie superalgebra-the two cases of the strong Macdonald conjecture. The challenge is to prove the isomorphism topologically without those results, or construct the trace directly from topology.

## Submitted Area

Algebraic topology, rational homotopy theory, representation theory, and Lie algebra cohomology

## Proposed Tracks

Algebra/Number Theory, Geometry

## Reference/source material

1. Yuri Berest, Ajay C. Ramadoss, and Wai-Kit Yeung, “Representation homology of simply connected spaces”, Journal of Topology 15 (2022), 692-744.

2. Susanna Fishel, Ian Grojnowski, and Constantin Teleman, “The strong Macdonald conjecture and Hodge theory on the loop Grassmannian”, Annals of Mathematics 168 (2008), 175-220.

3. Yuri Berest, Giovanni Felder, Sasha Patotski, Ajay C. Ramadoss, and Thomas Willwacher, “Representation homology, Lie algebra cohomology and the derived Harish-Chandra homomorphism”, Journal of the European Mathematical Society 19 (2017), 2811-2893.

## Desired outcome

A direct topological construction of the Drinfeld homomorphism that agrees with the existing map, or a topological proof that the Drinfeld homomorphism is an isomorphism for the specified spaces without using Fishel-Grojnowski-Teleman’s cohomology results. A full proof, translated through the known comparisons with Lie algebra cohomology, would provide an independent topological proof of both cases of the strong Macdonald conjecture. Fishel, Grojnowski, and Teleman originally proved it in full generality through a demanding algebraic argument.
