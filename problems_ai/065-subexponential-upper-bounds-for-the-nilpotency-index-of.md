## From the proposer

### Title/name of problem

Subexponential Upper Bounds for the Nilpotency Index of Associative Algebras

### Short description of problem (100 words or less)

Let A be an associative algebra generated by l elements and satisfying the polynomial identity x^d = 0. Its nilpotency index is the smallest integer k such that every product of k elements of A is zero.

Prove that the nilpotency index is less than Psi(d,d,l), where

Psi(n,d,l) = 2^18 · l · (nd)^(3 log_3(nd) + 13) · d^2.

Investigate possible improvements of this bound using combinatorics of words, n-divisibility, and Shirshov's height theorem.

### The result

Belov–Kharitonov (Corollary 1.5): an associative algebra generated by $l$ elements and satisfying $x^d=0$ has nilpotency index less than
$$\Psi(d,d,l)=2^{18}\,l\,(d^2)^{3\log_3(d^2)+13}\,d^{2}=2^{18}\,l\,d^{\,12\log_3 d+28}.$$
This follows from a statement about words (Theorem 10): for $d\ge n$, every word over $l$ letters of length at least $\Psi(n,d,l)$ contains a $d$-th power of a subword or is $n$-divisible, i.e. has the form $vu_1\cdots u_n$ with $u_1\succ\cdots\succ u_n$ lexicographically. They also bound the Shirshov height of non-$n$-divisible words by $\Phi(n,l)=2^{87}l\,n^{12\log_3 n+48}$. This answers Zelmanov's question (Dniester Notebook, 1993): the nilpotency class of the free 2-generated ring with $x^m=0$ grows subexponentially in $m$.

### How the known proof works

Structure as given in the key reference (Sections 1–5):
- $x^d=0$ implies an identity with coefficient 1 on $x_1\cdots x_d$ (its full linearization), so a $d$-divisible word is a combination of lexicographically smaller words; it suffices to bound words that are neither $d$-divisible nor contain a $d$-th power.
- Lemmas 2.1–2.4 give conditions for a period of length $d$ in a word that is not $n$-divisible and relate $n$-divisibility to the tails of the word.
- Following Latyshev, Dilworth's theorem splits a chosen set of tails into chains, which colour the tails.
- A hierarchical count comparing $p$-tails with $3p$-tails (Lemma 3.2) bounds the word length; the factor 3 produces the $\log_3$.
- Sections 4–5 bound the number of distinct periodic pieces (essential height) and give $\Phi$.

### Background

- Shirshov's height theorem (1957) implies that finitely generated algebras with $x^d=0$ are nilpotent, with primitive recursive bounds. Later height bounds: $l^{l^d}$ (Kolotov, 1982), exponential (Belov), refinements by Klein.
- Nagata–Higman (Dubnov–Ivanov 1943): in characteristic 0 or $p>d$ the index is below $2^d$, independent of $l$. Razmyslov proved $\le d^2$ in characteristic 0; Kuzmin proved $\ge d(d+1)/2$ and conjectured equality. For $0<p\le d$ the index is at least $l$.
- Lopatin (2012; his $C_{n,d}$ has $n$ = index, $d$ = generators): for $p>d/2$ the index is below $d^{\log_2(3l+2)+1}$ and below $4\cdot2^{d/2}\,l$, which beats $\Psi$ for small $d$ only.
