## From the proposer

### Title/name of problem

The Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis

### Short description of problem (100 words or less)

The Riemann hypothesis conjectures that all nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function have real part 1/2. A quasi-Riemann hypothesis is a weaker version of this conjecture, a zero-free half-plane of the form Re(s)>sigma, for some sigma < 1. Last month, OpenAI published two papers establishing quasi-Riemann, one asserting a zero-free region Re(s)>11/12 and the other a zero-free region Re(s)>7/8. Much of the proof is implicitly operating in the realm of Tate's Thesis, and subsequent extensions to Dedekind Zeta Functions by AxiomMath further solidify these connections.

### The result

OpenAI's Theorem 1.1 (30 September): every finite-order Hecke $L$-function over $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ and every Dirichlet $L$-function has no zero in $\operatorname{Re}s>7/8$ (the pole at $s=1$ allowed). In particular
$$\zeta(s)\neq0\qquad(\operatorname{Re}s>7/8),$$
the quasi-Riemann hypothesis. Corollary 1.2 bounds the least quadratic non-residue modulo $p$ by $C(\log p)^A$. The 5 October paper proves the same for $\operatorname{Re}s>11/12$ by a shorter route; its Corollary 1.2 is the prime number theorem in progressions with error $\ll x^{11/12}\log x$, uniformly for moduli $q\le x$.

### How the known proof works

- Reduction (11/12 paper, §§2–3): a power saving for smoothed Möbius sums $A_1(D)=\sum_{\mathfrak n}\mu(\mathfrak n)\nu(\mathfrak n)W(N\mathfrak n/D)$, twisted by a Hecke character $\nu$ of $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$, gives the zero-free half-plane. Embed $A_1$ in the family $A_u(D)$ twisted by sextic residue symbols $(u/n)_6$; a mean-square bound over $u$ (Proposition 3.1) and $A_{p^6}(D)\approx A_1(D)$ for many primes $p$ give $A_1(D)\ll D^{11/12+\varepsilon}$.
- Poisson summation in $u$ (§4): Gauss–Jacobi identities turn $\mu(n)$ times a sextic Gauss sum into cubic Gauss sums, which are Fourier coefficients of Kubota's cubic theta function (Patterson's formula).
- After completion by cubes (Dunn–Radziwiłł), the theta transformation gives dual sums twisted by quadratic characters, so the Goldmakher–Louvel quadratic large sieve applies; Möbius inversion removes the cube factors, and the large cube divisors are handled by iterating a recursive mean-square inequality (§§5–7).
- The 7/8 paper compares two representations of one completed cubic-theta sum, a cubic reflection (Proposition 5.1) and Poisson summation, whose row $u=1$ contains $1/L_F(s,\eta)$, inside a common continuation criterion (Proposition 2.1). Part I (§§3–11) proves 11/12 with a zero detector and the sextic large sieve; Part II (§§12–20) adds prime compensation, unequal scales and two moment estimates proved by induction (Lemmas 17.1, 18.1).
- Transfer: $\chi\circ N_{F/\mathbb Q}$ is a Hecke character whose $L$-function is $L(s,\chi)L(s,\chi\chi_{-3})$ up to finitely many Euler factors.

### Background

- Classical zero-free regions shrink toward $\operatorname{Re}s=1$ as height or conductor grow.
- The adelic version is in OpenAI's primitive-root paper (§§3–8). Cureton–Thorner (§2.3) list six ingredients of that argument: Tate's thesis supplies the Fourier analysis behind the Poisson evaluation and the functional equations, and Kazhdan–Patterson's cubic exceptional representation the theta function.
- Quanta Magazine (19 October 2026) reports that the experts it consulted largely agree the result is correct, that M. Radziwiłł can sketch the argument in half a page, and that write-ups by other mathematicians of 7 to 13 pages circulate privately, unpublished.
