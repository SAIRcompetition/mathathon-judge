# 77 · Topological Drinfeld homomorphism and the strong Macdonald conjecture

## From the proposer

- **Area:** Algebraic topology, rational homotopy theory, representation theory, and Lie algebra cohomology
- **Tracks:** Algebra/Number Theory, Geometry

### Description

For a simply connected space X and a complex reductive algebraic group G, the Drinfeld homomorphism maps a free graded-commutative algebra on selected Hodge components of circle-equivariant free-loop homology of X to G-invariant representation homology of X. Both sides have topological interpretations. Berest, Ramadoss, and Yeung prove this map is an isomorphism for specified spaces using Fishel, Grojnowski, and Teleman’s cohomology results for truncated current Lie algebras and a related Lie superalgebra-the two cases of the strong Macdonald conjecture. The challenge is to prove the isomorphism topologically without those results, or construct the trace directly from topology.

### Desired outcome

A direct topological construction of the Drinfeld homomorphism that agrees with the existing map, or a topological proof that the Drinfeld homomorphism is an isomorphism for the specified spaces without using Fishel-Grojnowski-Teleman’s cohomology results. A full proof, translated through the known comparisons with Lie algebra cohomology, would provide an independent topological proof of both cases of the strong Macdonald conjecture. Fishel, Grojnowski, and Teleman originally proved it in full generality through a demanding algebraic argument.

### The result

Let $X$ be simply connected and $G$ a complex reductive group whose Lie algebra has exponents $m_1,\dots,m_l$. Berest–Ramadoss–Yeung construct the Drinfeld homomorphism
$$\Lambda\Big(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}H^{S^1,(m_i)}_*(\mathcal LX;\mathbb C)\Big)\longrightarrow HR_*(X,G)^G$$
from Hodge components of reduced $S^1$-equivariant free loop space homology to $G$-invariant representation homology. Theorem 1.2: it is an isomorphism if $H^*(X;\mathbb Q)$ is generated by one element or freely generated by one even and one odd element (for example $S^{2n}$, $\mathbb{CP}^r$, $\mathbb{HP}^r$). Fishel–Grojnowski–Teleman proved the strong Macdonald conjecture: $H^\bullet(\mathfrak g[z]/z^n)$ is a free exterior algebra on $n\ell$ generators (Theorem A), and the restricted cohomology of $\mathfrak g[z,s]$ relative to $\mathfrak g$ is a free graded commutative algebra (Theorem B).

### How the known proof works

Structure of the proof of Theorem 1.2 (Theorem 4.5) in the key reference:
- Theorem 1.1 (most of Section 3, long and technical): representation homology of $X$ is Chevalley–Eilenberg (co)homology of a current Lie algebra $\mathfrak g(\bar A_X)$ built from the Sullivan model.
- Section 4: Drinfeld traces of invariant polynomials $P_i$, built with Chern–Simons forms, give the homomorphism on Lie–Hodge homology; Theorem 4.3 identifies it with an algebraic map $\Psi_*(A)$ of the Sullivan model $A$.
- If $H^*(X;\mathbb Q)$ is polynomial on one even class, or $X$ is rationally an odd sphere, the isomorphism reduces to classical facts about invariant polynomials.
- For $A=\mathbb C[z,s]$ with zero differential, Proposition 4.1, a refinement of Fishel–Grojnowski–Teleman's Theorem B, gives the isomorphism.
- For $H^*(X;\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q[z]/(z^{r+1})$, a filtration of the Quillen model gives a spectral sequence whose first page is the $\mathbb C[z,s]$ case, so this case also rests on Proposition 4.1.

### Background

- Hanlon proposed the truncated conjecture and proved it for $\mathfrak{gl}_n$; Feigin related it to the cohomology of $\mathfrak g[z,s]$.
- Fishel–Grojnowski–Teleman prove Theorem B via harmonic cocycles in the Koszul complex, for a metric coming from the Kähler geometry of the loop Grassmannian, and deduce Theorem A from Theorem B by Feigin's argument.
- Berest–Felder–Patotski–Ramadoss–Willwacher (2017) introduced derived representation schemes of Lie algebras and the derived Harish-Chandra homomorphism, and reinterpreted Macdonald's conjectures homologically.

### Where to start
